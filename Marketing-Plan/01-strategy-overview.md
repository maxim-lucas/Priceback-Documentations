# Strategy Overview

## What PriceBack is (for marketing purposes)

Scan your receipt or a shelf tag → PriceBack tracks the price → if it drops within the return/price-adjustment window, we tell you and help you claim the difference. Costco-first, expanding to other retailers. Money literally comes back to the user's pocket — this is the single most shareable fact about the product and should anchor every piece of content.

## Target users (v1)

1. **Costco power-shoppers** — already primed for "price adjustment" behavior (Costco has a generous 30-day policy most people don't use). This is the beachhead.
2. **Deal/frugal-living community** — r/frugal, r/PersonalFinanceCanada, coupon TikTok, "money saving mom" Instagram niche.
3. **Canadian suburban families** doing big-box grocery runs — secondary wave once Costco traction exists.

## Core growth thesis

People don't share budgeting apps. They share **proof of free money**. The growth engine is not "download our app" — it's "look how much I got back," where the screenshot/video of a real refund notification *is* the ad. Every piece of content strategy below optimizes for producing that receipt-refund moment as often and as visibly as possible.

## Three growth loops we're building

1. **Content loop (organic social):** Claude-run IG/TikTok posts real refund stats + educational "did you know Costco owes you money" hooks → drives installs → new users generate their own refund moments → some post organically (UGC) → loop compounds. See [02-social-media-strategy](02-social-media-strategy.md).
2. **Referral loop:** built-in "Invite a friend" already exists in the app (see [[profile-card-earn-redesign]] — InviteFriend sub-page). Marketing's job is to make the referral reward itself screenshot-worthy and to promote it inside content, not to build new product.
3. **Community-seeding loop:** partner with existing Costco/deal Facebook groups and subreddit mods (they already have the audience; we bring them a tool that makes their members money). Cheapest CAC available at launch.

## Non-negotiables from the product side

- Never promise a specific dollar amount or guaranteed refund in marketing copy — price drops are real but probabilistic; legal/compliance risk if we overclaim ([[price-drop-commission-and-policy-status]], [[verified-price-drop-notifications]]).
- Any claim about "verified" price drops should reflect the actual rule-of-3 crowd verification system, not an unverified single scan ([[verified-price-drop-notifications]]).
- All support/contact surfaces route to `@priceback.ca` addresses — never personal email in public-facing content ([[support-email-personal-fallback]]).
