# Creative Growth Tactics (beyond social content)

Ideas ranked by expected impact/effort for a pre-scale app trying to get a large user base quickly, Canada-first.

## High-priority, low-cost

1. **"Free money finder" receipt-scan challenge** — a launch stunt: post publicly "send us your last Costco receipt (redact card info), we'll tell you free if you're owed money" — Claude/team manually processes a batch during launch week, posts results (with consent) as proof-of-refund content. Turns cold audience into warm leads fast and generates a week of authentic content simultaneously.
2. **Facebook/Reddit group seeding** — identify the 10-15 largest Costco Canada / deal-hunting Facebook groups and relevant subreddits; post genuinely useful content (not ads) that happens to mention the tool when relevant. Group admins are often willing to pin a useful post if it's not spammy — worth a direct, honest outreach DM to a few large group admins offering early access codes.
3. **Referral loop amplification** — the in-app referral system already exists ([[profile-card-earn-redesign]]); make the referral reward itself the subject of a content piece ("I got $X just for inviting my mom") rather than only explaining it in help text.
4. **"Costco price adjustment" SEO/content play** — most people don't know Costco's 30-day return/price-adjustment policy exists. Own the search intent: a simple, well-written explainer page on the website ([[priceback-website-repo]]) targeting "Costco price adjustment policy Canada" — free organic search traffic, complements social.
5. **Micro-influencer seeding (not paid ads)** — DM 20-30 small Canadian frugal-living/deal TikTok creators (5k-50k followers) offering free early access + a simple affiliate/referral code; cheaper and higher-trust than a paid ad campaign, and creator content sourced this way is reusable as UGC on our own channels. The code system and how to price each deal: [10-creator-and-agency-partner-program](10-creator-and-agency-partner-program.md).

## Medium-priority

6. **Costco parking-lot/community-board flyers with QR code** — genuinely novel and rarely done for an app: a small printed flyer near Costco entrances/community boards ("Did Costco just drop the price on what you bought? Scan to check.") QR-linked straight to app store. Very cheap, hyper-targeted to exactly the intent moment (leaving Costco with a receipt in hand).
7. **Partnership pitch to Costco-adjacent bloggers/newsletter writers** (e.g. Canadian deal newsletters) — offer a co-branded "here's how much our readers got back this month" recurring feature; mutually beneficial content, no cash needed.
8. **Gamified milestone content** — public running counter ("PriceBack has returned $XX,XXX to Canadians so far") as a recurring social/website content piece — social proof compounds as the number grows, cheap to produce (pull from real aggregate data).

## Watch-list / later stage (needs more scale or budget first)

9. Paid TikTok/Meta ads once organic CAC and conversion are measured — don't spend before organic funnel is validated.
10. App Store / Play Store ASO pass (keywords, screenshots featuring real refund proof) — cheap, should happen alongside launch, not a growth "tactic" so much as hygiene.
11. Costco-corporate partnership conversation — high effort/high risk (Costco could see this as competitive with their own price-adjustment desk), only worth pursuing once user numbers make PriceBack impossible to ignore, not a launch tactic.

## What we're explicitly NOT doing

- No paid follower/engagement buying — kills algorithmic trust and violates platform ToS.
- No spam-posting affiliate links into Facebook groups without admin permission — burns community trust that's hard to rebuild.
- No guaranteed-refund-dollar-amount claims in any acquisition channel (compliance, see [01-strategy-overview](01-strategy-overview.md)).
